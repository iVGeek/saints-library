Andrew enters the Gospels as the first disciple — the first man Jesus called, and the first to recognize Him. A fisherman of Bethsaida and at first a disciple of John the Baptist, Andrew heard John point to Jesus and say, "Behold the Lamb of God," and he followed. His first act as a disciple was to go and find his brother: "We have found the Messiah," he told Simon, and he brought Simon Peter to Jesus. The Church has always remembered this — the man who came first, and whose first thought was to bring someone else.

## The Apostle of the East

The Gospels say little more of Andrew; tradition says a great deal. After Pentecost, Andrew is said to have carried the Gospel into Scythia — the lands north of the Black Sea, which is why Russia and the Slavic nations claim him — and then to Greece, preaching at Patras, founding churches, and, like Peter, meeting his death in the shape of the cross. The *Acts of Andrew*, written down in the early centuries, preserves the memory of an apostle who preached "to all the peoples of the land of Greece and of the land of the Scythians."

## The Cross of Saint Andrew

At Patras, condemned by the proconsul Aegeates, Andrew was sentenced to crucifixion. As he approached the cross, tradition says, he greeted it as a gift, asking that he not be hung on the same shape as his Lord — unworthy, he said, to die in the form of the Master's glory — and so his cross was fixed diagonally, on the X-shape that has carried his name and his banner ever since. He died after two days of hanging, still teaching those who gathered. Scotland adopted his saltire as its national flag; Russia honors him as her first apostle; and the Church keeps his feast at the very beginning of Advent, as the first-called leads the way to Christ.
