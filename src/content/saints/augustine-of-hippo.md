"No one can say anything worthy of God," Augustine once wrote. Yet no one has said more, and more beautifully, about God and the human heart than this North African bishop, whose *Confessions* — the autobiography of a soul in search of its Maker — is one of the greatest books in the Western tradition.

## The Restless Heart

Augustine was born in 354 at Thagaste in Roman North Africa, the son of a pagan father and a Christian mother, Monica, whose tears for his conversion became proverbial. Brilliant and ambitious, he pursued a career in rhetoric, and with it the pleasures and ambitions of the world. He took a concubine, fathered a son, and joined the fashionable heresy of Manichaeism — only to grow disillusioned with it. For years he searched: through the Platonists, through doubt, through the preaching of Saint Ambrose at Milan. In the summer of 386, sitting in a garden in Milan, he heard a child's voice chanting, "Take and read." He opened the scriptures at random and read Saint Paul's words to the Romans: "Put on the Lord Jesus Christ." His long search ended; in 387, on Easter night, Ambrose baptized him. His mother died shortly after, her lifelong prayer answered.

## The Bishop of Hippo

Returning to Africa, Augustine was ordained against his will and became bishop of the seaport of Hippo in 395. For thirty-five years he preached almost daily, wrote ceaselessly, and shepherded his flock through the collapse of the Roman world. His writings — the *Confessions*, the *City of God*, the treatises on grace, the Trinity, and scripture — made him the most influential theologian of the Latin Church, so much so that he is called the "Doctor of Grace."

## The City of God

When Rome fell in 410 and pagans blamed the Christians, Augustine answered in the *City of God*: the two cities — of the world and of God — mingle on earth, but their destinies are distinct, and only the city of God endures. He died on 28 August 430, as the Vandals besieged Hippo, having asked that the penitential psalms be written on the wall by his bed. He is buried — fittingly, for a man who spent a lifetime searching — with his mother Monica, whose prayer was the beginning of his peace.
