Ordained a priest in the diocese of Spoleto, Italy in 1216. He met and became one of the early spiritual students of Saint Francis of Assisi in 1219. Father Andrea was the first priest to join the Franciscans; he received the habit from Francis in 1223, was at the Saint's deathbed in 1226, attended the celebration of his canonization in 1228, and supervised the enshrining of the relics of Saint Francis. Andrea preached in Rome, in Padua, and locations in modern France. Known as a miracle worker, he is reported to have brought a dead man back to life in Reggio Emilia, Italy c.1240. Imprisoned twice between 1244 and 1247 as part of a theological dispute over the teachings of Saint Francis, and how to apply them to Franciscan life. Assigned by Saint Clare of Assisi to help with the reform of the Santa Maria di Vallegloria convent near Spello, Italy in 1248. He retired from public ministry to live as a hermit near Assisi, Italy, and in 1249 received a vision of the young Jesus.
