Benedictine monk in the monastery of San Nicolà l'Arena in Catania, Italy; he later served there as prior. Abbot of Santa Maria di Maniaci monastery and Santa Maria Nuova monastery in Monreale, Italy. Angelo was so strict about the Benedictine Rule in his houses that some of his brother monks threatened his life. In 1346 he rebuilt the monastery of San Martino delle Scale in Palermo, Italy, which had been destroyed by Saracens in the 9th century; it opened again on 13 January 1347 with six brothers, and Angelo became abbot of the house on 26 July 1352. He worked with the sick during plague outbreaks in 1348 and 1362. Late in life, he retired to live for a while as a prayerful hermit in a cell near his monastery of San Martino; Angelus then returned to lead the monastery, and brought it to a period of great fame for the piety of the brothers.
