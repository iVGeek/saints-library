Raised in a Christian family, Camille lost his faith as a young man, but came back enthusiastically when he had a conversion experience at age 22. He studied at the French seminary in Rome, Italy, and was ordained a priest of the diocese of Chambéry, Savoie, France on 26 May 1866.
Father Camille had planned to devote himself to ministery to working men, but Chambéry was struck by a cholera epidemic which left many children orphaned. At first Camille housed them at his home, but soon there were so many that he needed help. With a donation from the Count of Boigne, Father Camille founded the Bocage orphanage and school with 125 children, and supervised it the rest of his life. At his death, his nephew Ernest took over. He handed it off to the Salesians of Don Bosco in 1954, and they continue the good work of the Bocage today.
