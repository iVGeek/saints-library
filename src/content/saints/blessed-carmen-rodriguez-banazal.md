The daughter of Francisco and Rosa, Carmen was educated in the local city school. In her teens, she became to feel a call to religious life, and joined the Daughters of Charity on 16 August 1897. After training at the Hospital of the Incurrables in Madrid, Spain, Sister Carmen was assigned to serve as a school teacher in Bétera, Spain. She served there for years, was known for her piety and hardwork, and in 1935 was chosen superior of the Daughters community.
At the start of the Spanish Civil War in July 1936, the Daughters' house was attacked, the Order outlawed, the community dispersed; the town mayor issued an order that no one was to help them in any way. Carmen and four of her religious sisters took refuge in the house of an old student, but were spotted by the militia and forced to leave for Valencia. However, the government in Bétera, including that same mayor, issued orders for their imprisonment and execution for refusing to renounce their faith. The five were imprisoned at their school for a period, and then executed. Martyr.
