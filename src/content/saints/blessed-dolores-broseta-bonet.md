One of six children born to Joaquín and María, a laboring family; two of her siblings died as small children. She was educated by the Daughters of Charity, was a member of the Daughters of Mary of the Miraculous Medal, and in her late teens began to feel a call to religious life. She became a Daughters of Charity postulant, but health reasons prevented her joining the Order. She returned to Bétera and worked in the College-Asylum as a Daughter of Mary, helping in the children's classes and in the embroidery workshop. In 1925, when her mother died, Dolores moved into the Asylum, still not a member of the Daughters, but living their life and helping them with everything.
At the start of the Spanish Civil War in July 1936, the Daughters' house was attacked, the Order outlawed, the community dispersed; the town mayor issued an order that no one was to help them in any way. Dolores and four of her religious sisters took refuge in the house of an old student, but were spotted by the militia and forced to leave for Valencia. However, the government in Bétera, including that same mayor, issued orders for their imprisonment and execution for refusing to renounce their faith. The five were imprisoned at their school for a period, and then executed. Martyr.
