The daughter of Idelfonso and Juana, Estefanía grew up in a farming family. She was educated at home, then moved to Valencia where her aunt, a member of the Daughters of Charity, helped her enter the Order on 21 November 1896. Assigned to Bétera, Spain, she served as a teacher for 39 years, noted for his kindness and patience with children, devotion to the Rule and spirituality of the Daughters.
At the start of the Spanish Civil War in July 1936, the Daughters' house was attacked, the Order outlawed, the community dispersed; the town mayor issued an order that no one was to help them in any way. Estefanía and four of her religious sisters took refuge in the house of an old student, but were spotted by the militia and forced to leave for Valencia. However, the government in Bétera, including that same mayor, issued orders for their imprisonment and execution for refusing to renounce their faith. The five were imprisoned at their school for a period, and then executed. Martyr.
