After basic education, Honoratus studied architecture at the School of Fine Arts in Warsaw, Poland. During his school years, he abandoned the faith. In 1846 he was imprisoned by the Tsarist police for political reasons; he feared a death sentence, and nearly died of typhus. This led a crisis and re-examination of his faith, which led to his return to the Church and a call to religious life.
Honoratus joined the Capuchin Friars Minor in Warsaw in 1848, and was ordained a priest on 27 December 1852. He was dedicated to preaching, writing, spiritual direction, penance and confession, and prison ministry. He founded 26 religious congregations, 18 of which continue their good work today; see the list in the Patronage section below. In 1863, religious orders were outlawed in Russia which caused Father Honoratus to be transferred to Zahroczym and then to Nowe-Miasto, Poland. He opposed the Tsarists who wanted to separate the Polish church from the Vatican. Chosen general commissioner of the Capuchins under Russian domination in 1895. In 1905, due to health problems, Honoratus withdrew from parish ministry but continued spiritual direction by an extensive correspondence with thousands of his letters surviving.
