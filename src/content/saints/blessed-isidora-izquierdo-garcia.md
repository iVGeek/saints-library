The daughter of Esteban and Felicitas, Isidora was baptized at the age of eight days; she was educated at home, and then at the College of La Milagrosa of Rabé de las Calzadas in Burgos, Spain. In her teens, she felt a call to religious life, joined the Daughters of Charity, did her initial training at the Saint John hospital in Burgos, and then at the mother house in Madrid, arriving on 15 November 1901. Sister Isidora was served as a teacher and catechist in Bétera, Spain for 35 years, preparing class after class for their First Communion, and running a small farm to feed the children. Because of her commitment and care, her students often came back to her as adults to seek advice in life and spirituality.
At the start of the Spanish Civil War in July 1936, the Daughters' house was attacked, the Order outlawed, the community dispersed; the town mayor issued an order that no one was to help them in any way. Isidora and four of her religious sisters took refuge in the house of an old student, but were spotted by the militia and forced to leave for Valencia. However, the government in Bétera, including that same mayor, issued orders for their imprisonment and execution for refusing to renounce their faith. The five were imprisoned at their school for a period, and then executed. Martyr.
