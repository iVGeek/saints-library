A wild and undisciplined child, Jaime was educated from age 12 by the Salesians; in his teens he abandoned school to work as a mechanic. Though he enjoyed secular life, and was known as a wild and undisciplined young man, Jaime began to see the misery to which such a life led his co-workers, and decided he wanted better. He returned to the Salesians and asked to join as a co-adjutor, that is, a religious brother with no plans for the priesthood; he made his final vows in 1932, and taught in the technical and occupational schools run by the Society. He attended the canonization recognition of Saint John Bosco. Martyred in the Spanish Civil War.
