Jean-Nicolas joined the Jesuits in Nancy, France on 28 September 1728. Priest. After studying at the University of Pont-à-Mousson, he taught philosophy at colleges in Dijon, Auxerre, Autun, Stasbourg, and Pont-à-Mousson. Prefect of studies in Reims in 1757. Superior of the house of Saint-Mihiel in the diocese of Verdun in 1761. When the Jesuits were suppressed in Lorraine in 1786, Father Jean-Nicholas stayed at Saint-Mihiel, and served as chaplain to a nearby convent. WHen all religious orders were dissolved during the anti–Catholic persecutions of the French Revolution, he moved to Verdun.
Arrested on 28 October 1793 for the crime of being a priest, he was ordered to be deported, he was imprisoned at Bar le Duc, France for six months. On 15 April 1794, barely able to walk, he was imprisoned on the Washington, a ship in the harbor of Rochefort, France and left to die. One of the Martyrs of the Hulks of Rochefort.
