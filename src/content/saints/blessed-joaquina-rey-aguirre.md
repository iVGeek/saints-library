The daughter of Francisco, a commercial real estate agent, and Jerónima, Joaquina was baptized at the age of six days. At the age of 30, she joined the Daughters of Charity, training at the La Princesa de Madrid hospital, and then the Daughters motherhouse. She was assigned to the Casa de Beneficencia in Valencia, Spain where she served as a teacher, inspector and administrative supervisor for ten years. She was known as a dedicated, responsible member of the Daughters, helpful to any who needed support in their work.
At the start of the Spanish Civil War in July 1936, the Daughters' house was attacked, the Order outlawed, the community dispersed; Sister Joaquina and Blessed Victoria Arregui Guinea hid with a family in Foyos, Spain. They were soon located, imprisoned, and then sentenced to death by the local Communist committee; Fathers José Ruiz and Antonio Good were condemned with them. Sister Joaquina, though she knew the decision had already been made, calmly argued against the actions of the Communists. When one of her would-be executioners became physically abusive, she snatched his weapon away; she then apologized for her actions, asked one of the condemned priests for absolution, returned the weapon, and forgave her killers. Martyr.
