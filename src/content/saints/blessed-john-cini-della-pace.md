A soldier in the army of the Republic of Pisa, Italy. A bit wild by nature, he was imprisoned at one point for the 8 October 1296 attack on Archbishop Teodorico Ranieri of Pisa. While serving his time, Giovanni had a conversion experience, and when he was released he became a Franciscan teritary and concentrated on a life of penance.
Beginning in 1305, he led the Pious House of Mercy, a charity devoted to the poorest of the poor; he began the practice of taking food and alms under cover of darkness to people who were too ashamed of their poverty to come get the supplies. Giovanni later became a hermit at the Porta della Paca in Pisa. His reputation for piety attracted several young men as would-be spiritual students, and he organizaed them into the Fraticelli or Franciscan Tertiary Hermits, a congregation that has since gone extinct. He spent his final years in a walled cell near the Porta, receiving Communion and water, giving spiritual advice.
