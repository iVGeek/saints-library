The second of eight children born to the farm family of José María and Tomasa Gorosterratzu y Jaunarena; José was Baptized on the day he was born. From his family he learned farming, shepherding, and praying the rosary in the fields. Though he felt a call to the priesthood in his early teens, his inability to speak Spanish well (his family spoke Basque) slowed his training. He worked as a carpenter, joined the Redemptorist on 8 September 1895, and continued his studies, finally being ordained a priest in the diocese of Cuenca, Spain on 28 September 1903.
He taught science and philosophy at the Redemptorist seminary. In 1913 he was assigned to serve as a missioner in both Basque and Spanish in the Pamplona, Spain region. In 1933 he was assigned to serve as a parish priest in Cuenca where he was known for his personal prayer life, for his zeal for pastoral work, and a much sought after confessor and spiritual guide. Away from pastoral life, he was known for his background in history, concentrating on Basque history and culture; he had been given permission to use the Vatican Archives for something he was working on at the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War. He went briefly into hiding from the anti–Christian Communist militia, but was given up, and after the usual demands that he deny his faith, he was murdered. Martyr.
