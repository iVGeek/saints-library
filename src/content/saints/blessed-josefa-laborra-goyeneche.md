The daughter of Francisco and Javier, Josefa was raised in a pious farm family with three brothers. She was educated at the College of the Immaculate, a school run by the Daughters of Charity. Member of the Daughters of Mary. She joined the Daughters of Charity on 18 March 1881, training at the hospital in Madrid, Spain and the hospice in Cuenca, Spain. Superior of the College-Asylum in Bétera, Valencia, Spain in July 1900. Superior of the hospital in Murcia in 1911, the staff, families and community in Bétera missed her so much that she was soon re-assigned there.
At the start of the Spanish Civil War in July 1936, the Daughters' house was attacked, the Order outlawed, the community dispersed; the town mayor issued an order that no one was to help them in any way. Josefa and four of her religious sisters took refuge in the house of an old student, but were spotted by the militia and forced to leave for Valencia. However, the government in Bétera, including that same mayor, issued orders for their imprisonment and execution for refusing to renounce their faith. The five were imprisoned at their school for a period, and then executed. Martyr.
