The third of six children born to the peasant family of Josè and Marcela, she was educated at home, and then at the boarding school run by the Daughters of Charity in Alberique, Spain. A member of the Daughters of Mary, Josefa felt a call to religious life, and joined the Daughters of Charity in Madrid on 30 November 1925. She served in the Provincial Hospital of Valencia, caring for elderly patients, and then began studying to be a nurse. She made her profession in 1936, not long before the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War. In the summer of 1936, the Communist forces outlawed the Daughters, and dispersed all the sisters; Josefa moved back Alberique to live with her parents. Her blood sister, Natalia, was pregnant, and the father had been threatened with death by the Communists for refusing to renounce his faith; Josefa offered to take his place, but was refused. The militia then seized the pregnant Natalia; Josefa again offered to take their place, and this time the Communists accepted. Martyr.
