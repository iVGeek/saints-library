The second of six children born to Giuseppe Talamoni, a hat maker, and Maria Angelica Sala; Luigi was baptized on the day of his birth. He was ordained a priest in the archdiocese of Milan, Italy on 4 March 1871. Teacher at the Collegio San Carlo in Milan; one of his students was the future Pope Pius XI. In 1875 he was assigned to Monza, Italy to teach at the local high school, preach, serve as spiritual director, and concentrate on bringing the faithful back to the confessional.
Being so active in the community, Father Luigi became involved in civil politics. In 1893 he was elected to the Monza city council. He served for several years, working to improve public services and support for education at all levels. He helped support small businesses and basic services to the poor. However, the rise of Fascism led to his resignation from political office. Co-founder of the Suore Misericordine di San Gerardo (Congregation of the Merciful Sisters of Saint Gerard) on 25 March 1891 for the care and education of orphans and abandoned children. The Sisters continue their good work today in Italy, Switzerland and Togo.
