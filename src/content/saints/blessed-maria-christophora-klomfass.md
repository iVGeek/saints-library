The daughter of August and Rozalia Such, Marta's mother was a Franciscan tertiary, and theirs was a pious home. In 1918, at age 15, the girl began studying tailoring in Biskupiec, Poland. There she came to know the Dominican Sisters of Saint Catherine; in 1922, at age 19, Marta travelled to Braniewo, Poland and asked to be admitted to the Sisters. She took the name Sister Maria Christophora, and was sent to nursing school in Królewiec, Poland. She received her nursing certification in 1926, made her first vows in the Sisters in 1928, and her solemn vows in 1931. She served as a nurse in the Polish cities of Szabruk, Frombork, Bartag, and Barczewo. In 1939 she was assigned to Saint Mary's Hospital in Olsztyn where she served through World War II. In early 1945 the Nazis retreated, the Red Army occupied the city, and civilians were evacuated – but the Sisters, including Sister Christophora, stayed to care for the sick and children still in hospital. The Soviet troops overran the hospital, robbing, raping and killing. This included the Sisters who were beaten and raped until they fought back – and then they were killed. Sister Christophora was the first of the them to die. Martyr.
