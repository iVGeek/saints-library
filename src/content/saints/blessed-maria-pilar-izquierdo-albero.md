The third of five children born to Mariano, a baker, and Paola. Theirs was a poor family, and Maria was known from early youth for her love of God and her desire to serve those even poorer than herself. As a young woman, she worked at a shoe factory to help support her family. In 1926 she fell from a street car and fractured her hip. In 1927 she developed cysts that blinded and paralyzed her. Thus began years of shuttling between hospitals and her room in the attic. Maria devoted her time in enforced isolation to prayer, in supporting people's faith during the persecutions of the Spanish Civil War, and looking for ways that she could bring works of mercy to the world.
On the Feast of the Immaculate Conception, 8 December 1939, Maria was miraculously healed of the cysts, paralysis and vision problems that had confined her. Soon after, she and a group of young, like-minded people moved to Madrid, Spain to begin an apostolate to the poor. She received diocesan approval to found the Missionaries of Jesus and Mary, but experienced great operational difficulty, and the Missionaries were dissolved. In 1942 she received diocesan approval to try again, this time founding the Pious Union of Missionaries of Jesus, Mary and Joseph. Two years into the work, her health began to fail again, she was forced out of leadership, and withdrew from the Union completely in 1944. She died in 1945, but the work she started continued, and in 1948 the Union received diocesan approval under the name Missionary Work of Jesus and Mary; they continue their good work today with 24 houses in Spain, Colombia, Ecuador, Venezuela, Mexico, Italy, Mozambique and Indonesia.
