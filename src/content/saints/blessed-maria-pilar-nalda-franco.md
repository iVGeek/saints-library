The daughter of Manuel, a physician, and Josefa, a housewife, Pilar was a member of the Daughters of Mary in her youth, and when old enough, joined the Daughters of Charity. She studied at the Santa Isabel hospital in Jerez de la Frontera, Spain, and entered her novitiate on 6 October 1889. Sister Justa Domínguez de Vidaurreta served as her teacher in general topics, life in the Daughters, and the profession of teacher. Sister Maria Pilar taught at the Hospital-Asylum of Mondoñedo and the Psychiatric Hospital of Leganés. In 1900, as general education for children became mandatory, taught in Catholic schools in Cádiz, Spain, then Dos Hermanas in Sevilla, Spain, and then in Bétera, Valencia, Spain. She was known as an enthusiastic teacher, dedicated especially to working with the poorest children.
At the start of the Spanish Civil War in July 1936, the Daughters' house was attacked, the Order outlawed, the community dispersed; the town mayor issued an order that no one was to help them in any way. María Pilar and four of her religious sisters took refuge in the house of an old student, but were spotted by the militia and forced to leave for Valencia. However, the government in Bétera, including that same mayor, issued orders for their imprisonment and execution for refusing to renounce their faith. The five were imprisoned at their school for a period, and then executed. Martyr.
