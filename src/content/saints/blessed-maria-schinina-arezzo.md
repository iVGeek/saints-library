Born to the Italian nobility, the fifth of eight children of Giambattista Schininà and Rosalia Arezzo. Though she had not been a particularly pious child or young woman, her father's death in 1865 caused Maria to re-examine her life and the best way to live it, and she had something of a conversion experience. When her youngest brother married in 1874, Maria began implementing her plan for a new life. She stayed at the family home to care for her mother, and when able, went into the streets to care for the poor and the sick. She joined the Pius Union of the Sisters of Mary in 1875, and in 1877 was chosen its director. Her mother died in 1884, and Maria was able to throw herself completely into her vocation. In 1885 she began organizing the group that would become the Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus of Ragusa (Suore del Sacro Cuore di Gesù di Ragusa) with a mission to care for the sick, poor, and abandoned. The Sisters made their first profession on 9 May 1889, received a decree of papal praise by Pope Pius XI on 27 November 1936, and a decree of full papal approval by Pope Pius XII on 11 March 1946. They continue their good work today with hundreds of religious sisters working at dozens of houses in Italy, Canada and Poland.
