One of eight children born to Zacarías and Antonia, owners of a pastry shop, Martina was known to be an intelligent, courageous, and strong young woman. She became engaged to a young man, then realized her calling to religious life and joined the Daughters of Charity instead. She worked at the hospital in Valladolid, Spain, and began studying at the Daughters' center in Madrid, Spain on 26 February 1896. Sister Martin served at the Hospice and College of the Miraculous Medal of Zamora; Segorbe hospital; Provincial House of Madrid; Doker de Melilla Hospital, and the Hospital, Schools and Charity Canteen of Segorbe; she served as local superior, assistant or provincial vicar, and organizer of the military hospitals of North Africa. In all her positions she was distinguished by her courage and audacity in favor of the poor, a firm faith, an open disposition, eager to do good to all, and capable of doing it.
At the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War, the Daughters were outlawed and expelled from working in the hospitals. The sisters moved in with an person who had been one of their students, lived as though they were under house arrest, and were routinely threatened with death by the Communist militia members. Seeing that martyrdom was a very real possibility, Sister Martina encouraged the her Sisters to prepare themselves. They wrote confessions and passed them along to a priest who was in hiding in a nearby house; he gave them all absolution through the window of his house. Martyr.
