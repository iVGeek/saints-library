The sixth of seven children born to Giovanni Medda and Priama Cogoni Zedda, Giovanni's father died when the boy was five, his mother then he was 13, and he spent much of his youth living with his adult sister who sent him into the fields to work the farm and watch the cattle. He made his First Communion in 1896, and it had a profound effect on him. For years he felt a call to religious life; a severe illness apparently galvanized his determination, and in March 1911, at age 29, he joined the Franciscan Friars Minor Capuchins at Cagliari, Italy as a third order oblate, taking the name Nicola; he made his solemn profession on 16 February 1919. For over a decade he served as a cook and then kitchen supervisor in houses in Sanluri, Sassari and Oristano, but in 1924 he was assigned to beg in the streets to support his house in Cagliari; he spent the rest of his life, 34 years, in this work. In addition to the money collected, being a beggar was a way to talk about the faith to people in the street. Though he was snubbed by many people for being a beggar, Brother Nicola became so well known for his holy wisdom that others sought him ought for his counsel and spiritual direction. He was asked to visit sick in hospital, there were reports of healings, and he always encouraged people to care for the poor. He spent World War II caring for the people who had been displaced and abandoned.
