In 1430, Cozzando joined the Servants of Mary at the convent of Sant Alessandro in Brescia, Italy, taking the name Paolo. He studied theology there and in Paris, France, and in 1450 received a doctorate in the field. Ordained a priest, he became an influential preacher. Prior of the Sant Alessandro convent in 1454. Vicar-general of the Servites in 1457. Vicar-general of the Congregation of the Osservanza in 1476. Assigned by Pope Sixtus IV to reform several houses; he worked with Blessed Tommaso Vitali, Blessed Bartolomeo Pinzocaro, and Blessed Bonaventura Tornielli to take the Servants back to a more active spirituality. Father Paolo was considered for the bishopric of Pesaro, Italy; when he learned of this idea, Paolo retired to live the rest of his life as a hermit on Monte Granaro to avoid such a public vocation.
