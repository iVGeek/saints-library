Benedictine monk at the San Liberatore monastery in Maiella mountains of Abruzzo Apennines, Italy. Feeling a call to a life of penance, he became a hermit in a cave near the monastery. Legend says that he got the “del Lupo” (“of the Wolf”) nickname when he miraculously tamed an aggressive wolf in the wild area outside Manoppello, Italy. Like many other hermits, his reputation for holiness spread, attracted many would-be spiritual students, and to deal with them he founded the San Pietro di Vallebona monastery near Manoppello; in 1149 the house associated with the Abbey of Santa Maria di Pulsano. For 40 years Stefano served as a monk and spiritual director, leaving the administration of the house to a series of priors. However, in 25 October 1188 Pope Clement III issued a special privilege to the house, elevated it to an abbey, and Stefano began service as its first abbot.
