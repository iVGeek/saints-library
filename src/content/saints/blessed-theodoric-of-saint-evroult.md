Educated by his uncle and godfather, Abbot Theodoric of Jumièges Abbey, the younger Theodoric became a Benedictine monk, then prior, then rector of the Jumièges monastic school. In 1050 he led a group of brother monks to re-claim the vacant Abbey of Saint-Evroult (in modern Saint-Evroult-Notre-Dame-du-Bois), Onche, Normandy (in modern France), and serve as its abbey. Theodoric's piety, his zeal for religious life, and his dedication to the Benedictine Rule led to the house flourishing again, and becoming a leading spiritual and educational light in the region. He encouraged and supported his monks in their studies, insured the work of their school and scriptorium. At one point he was accused of ignoring the administration and material concerns of the abbey, and offered to resign, but his bishop supported him and refused to accept the resignation. However, Theodoric was allowed to retire from his duties in 1057, and went on pilgrimage to the Holy Lands, dying on the trip.
