The daughter of Venancio, a farmhand, and Liboria, Victoria was educated by the Daughters of Charity, was a member of the Daughters of Mary of the Miraculous Medal, and as a girl developed a love of prayer, Marian devotion, and service to the poor. Feeling a call to religious life, she joined the Daughters of Charity on 17 March 1921, studying at the Provincial Hospital of Pamplona, and the Daughters' house in Madrid. As she was noted for her artistic needlework, she was assigned to the Casa Beneficencia in Valencia for the embroidery and sewing workshops; she taught many young women, and they produced works for use in many churches in Spain and foreign countries.
At the start of the Spanish Civil War in July 1936, the Daughters' house was attacked, the Order outlawed, the community dispersed; Sister Victoria and Blessed Joaquina Rey Aguirre hid with a family in Foyos, Spain. They were soon located, imprisoned, and then sentenced to death by the local Communist committee; Fathers José Ruiz and Antonio Good were condemned with them. Sister Victoria made sure she received absolution and the Eucharist from one of the condemned priests, and then died shouting, “Long live Christ the King!”. Martyr.
