Her father died on a pilgrimage to Jerusalem, and her mother soon after. Pilgrim at age 16 to Saint James de Compostella in Spain. On Ascension Day in 1248, she decided to withdraw from the world, and moved into a closed cell to live as a hermitess and anchorite near the Augustinian church and convent in Sankt Florian, Austria. She left it briefly in 1275 when the city was invaded by the forces of Rudolph of Habsburg, but otherwise lived there for her remaining 40 years. Her reputation for piety and wisdom spread, and a steady stream of people, lay and religious, high status and low, came to her cell window, seeking advice and prayers. Saint Agnes of Prague asked her to help establish a convent in Italy, but Wilburgis preferred her life of prayer, penance and devotion.
