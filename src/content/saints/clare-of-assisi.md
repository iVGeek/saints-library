On Palm Sunday 1212, in the church of San Damiano outside Assisi, a young noblewoman named Clare stepped forward to receive her palm — and that night slipped away from her father's house to the little chapel where Francis and his brothers prayed. There Francis cut her hair and clothed her in a rough habit. She was eighteen; within a decade she would be abbess of a community that still lives by her spirit.

## A Radical Choice

Clare was the eldest daughter of a noble Assisan family, promised a good marriage. Instead she chose poverty. Francis placed her with a community of Benedictine nuns and then in the church of San Damiano, which he had repaired years earlier. Around her gathered other women — including her own sister Agnes — and the community of the "Poor Ladies" (the Poor Clares) was born. At twenty-one, against her wishes, Clare was made abbess. For forty years she governed San Damiano with gentleness and iron fidelity to the ideal of "the privilege of poverty": the sisters owned nothing, held nothing in common beyond what they needed, and trusted entirely in God.

## The Power of the Blessed Sacrament

Clare's name is bound to one of the Church's most dramatic stories. In 1240, while Frederick II's Saracen troops were sacking Assisi, they came to the walls of San Damiano. Clare, gravely ill, had herself carried to the wall holding the ciborium with the Blessed Sacrament. The attackers fled. Later, on the feast of Saint Agatha, the whole city of Assisi was saved in the same manner, and the danger passed. It is for this that she is patroness of those who watch by night and of those who guard the Blessed Sacrament.

## The Rule and the Good Death

Clare wrote a Rule of life — remarkable in the thirteenth century — insisting on absolute poverty, and won from the popes its approval without compromise. In 1253, as she lay dying, Pope Innocent IV came to her bedside to bring her the final confirmation of the Rule. She died on 11 August 1253, holding the cross, and was canonized two years later. At her deathbed stood the friars and her sisters — and, as the legend of her last days tells, Francis, her father in God.
