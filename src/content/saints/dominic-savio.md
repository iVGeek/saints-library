Dominic Savio is the youngest non-martyr saint in the history of the Church — a boy of fourteen whose death in 1857 shocked his teacher, Don Bosco, into tears. Born in 1842 at San Giovanni di Riva in Piedmont, he received his First Holy Communion at seven and immediately kept a promise to his parish priest: "I will never commit sin." His family moved to Turin in 1854, and the twelve-year-old, with his father's permission, walked into the Oratory of Don Bosco and asked to join.

## The Saint Before He Was Old

At the Oratory Dominic Savio was a student of Don John Bosco, and the teacher recognized in the pupil a holiness that went beyond his years. Dominic wrote in his diary: "I was born for something great: to be a saint." He became the altar server who would arrive earliest and stay longest; the boy who counseled his companions against sin and who kept a list of his spiritual resolutions — "to give God no offense," "to frequent the sacraments," "to be a saint." Don Bosco tried to dampen the boy's fervor; the boy smiled and kept going. In 1856 his health began to fail, and Don Bosco sent him to the country to recover; he died on 9 March 1857, at fourteen, saying: "What a wonderful thing heaven is!"

## The Boy Saint

When Pius XII canonized him in 1954, he said of Dominic Savio: "Here is a saint who died at fourteen — and what he did, you can do." The Church's youngest non-martyr saint is its most compelling answer to the idea that holiness is for old people: Dominic Savio was a saint before his voice broke, and his feast on May 6 is kept by schools and parishes across the world as proof that you can be young and heroic, and that the grace of God does not wait for years.
