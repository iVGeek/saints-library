On 22 February 1931, in the convent at Płock, a young nun named Faustina Kowalska saw Jesus clothed in a white garment, one hand raised in blessing and the other touching his breast, from which two great rays streamed — one pale, one red. "Paint an image according to the pattern you see," He told her, "with the inscription: Jesus, I trust in You." She was twenty-five, a simple, unlettered Polish sister of Our Lady of Mercy, born Helena Kowalska at Głogowiec in 1905, the third of ten children of a poor farming family. She had almost no education and no learning; what she had was obedience and the diary in which she wrote down, over the last years of her short life, the messages of mercy she was given.

## The Diary

The Diary — nearly 700 pages of conversations with Jesus, spiritual struggles, and instructions — is one of the great documents of twentieth-century spirituality. In it Jesus tells her: "I desire mercy... which is the greatest attribute of God," and gives her the prayers of the Divine Mercy: the chaplet, the image, the feast on the Sunday after Easter. It records her own battles too — the illness that was to kill her, the darkness and dryness of spirit she called the "dark night," the petty suspicions of her superiors, and always the trust: "Jesus, I trust in You." Theologians who read it were staggered; the message of the Chaplet, "For the sake of His sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world," has become one of the most-prayed devotions of the Church.

## The Feast of Mercy

She died at Kraków in 1938, of tuberculosis, at thirty-three — the same age her Lord was when He died. She was buried in the convent cemetery, and few took notice. Then the devotion spread: the image, the chaplet, the Divine Mercy novena, carried by the Polish church and, above all, by a Polish pope, Karol Wojtyła, who as John Paul II canonized her in 2000 and on the same day declared the Second Sunday of Easter — the Sunday of mercy — the feast of Divine Mercy Sunday. The world now knows what the world then mocked: that God's mercy is poured out where people trust Him, and that the Church's newest message was given to a poor, sick, hidden nun — who simply wrote down what Jesus asked her to write.
