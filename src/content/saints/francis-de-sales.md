Francis de Sales is the saint who proved that gentleness can do what force cannot. Born at Thorens in Savoy in 1567, he was the eldest of twelve children, educated at Paris and Padua, ordained priest, and sent — against all hope — to win back the Protestant Chablais, a valley that had gone over to Calvin and refused to listen to a Catholic priest. He went anyway, walking the mountains in winter, leaving pamphlets on doorsteps, preaching in the fields, and answering the hatred with a patience that slowly, over years, brought most of the valley back to the faith.

## The Gentle Pastor

In 1602 he became bishop of Geneva — the see that had been Calvin's — and governed it for twenty years with the same extraordinary gentleness. He wrote books: the Introduction to the Devout Life, the first book of spirituality written for laypeople, telling them that holiness was not for monks but for anyone who loved God in the kitchen, at the office, in the ordinary work of the day. With Jane Frances de Chantal he founded the Visitation Order — a community of women who would visit the sick and the poor, without enclosure, in the streets and houses of the city. He died in 1622, on his way to France, worn out by the charity that would not let him rest.

## The Doctor of Gentleness

The Church made him a Doctor in 1923, and the title — "Doctor of the Devout Life" — is itself a sermon: here is a saint whose whole teaching is that you can be holy where you are, without leaving the world behind. His book has never gone out of print; his phrase "Be who God meant you to be, and you will set the world on fire" has passed into the language; and his Salesians — the order Don Bosco founded in his name — have carried his gentle spirit to the ends of the earth.
