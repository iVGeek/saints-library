Francis Xavier is the model of the missionary — the man who left everything behind and went to the ends of the earth, and who died on a rock in the South China Sea, waiting for a boat that never came. Born in 1506 at the Castle of Xavier in Navarre, he was a young scholar of Paris when Ignatius of Loyola captured his heart; and when Ignatius founded the Society of Jesus, Xavier was one of the seven who took their vows at Montmartre. But it was the conversion of the Indies that claimed him, and in 1542 he sailed for Goa, never to return to Europe.

## The Apostle of the Indies

His mission was impossible by any human reckoning — one priest, alone, with no resources, arriving in a world of millions who had never heard the name of Christ. In three years he preached across southern India, from Travancore to Ceylon, baptizing thousands with a simplicity that astonished the converts: he learned their languages, visited their villages, and made the sign of the cross over their sick. The missionaries who followed him would find churches where he had been, and congregations who could still recite the prayers he had taught them in their own tongue.

## The Edge of China

In 1549 he reached Japan, the first European to do so, and spent two years learning the language, debating the Buddhist monks, and founding a Christian community that would survive the centuries. When he turned to China — the great prize, the civilization that governed the East — the door was closed to foreigners. He waited on the small island of Shangchuan, off the Chinese coast, for a boat to smuggle him in. On 3 December 1552 he was found dead in his hut, gazing at the coast of China he had never reached. He was forty-six. His body was brought back to Goa and rests there still, incorrupt, in the Basilica of Bom Jesus — a saint who died at the threshold of the land he could not enter, and whose mission would take root across the Pacific for centuries after his death.
