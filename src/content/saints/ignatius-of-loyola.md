Ignatius of Loyola was a soldier before he was a saint. Born in 1491 in the Basque castle of Loyola, he pursued a life of arms, ambition, and courtly romance until a cannonball shattered his leg at the siege of Pamplona in 1521. Laid up in his family castle, he asked for the romances he loved — but the house had only the life of Christ and the lives of the saints. He read them, and, as he later said, "began to be stirred in his heart."

## The Cave at Manresa

As he recovered, Ignatius renounced his old life, hung his sword before the image of the Virgin at Montserrat, and withdrew to a cave at Manresa, near the river Cardoner. There, over months of prayer, penance, and spiritual struggle, he received the deep graces that he shaped into the *Spiritual Exercises* — a thirty-day program of meditation, examination of conscience, and discernment of spirits that became one of the most influential works of spiritual direction in the Church. Its aim is summed up in its motto: "finding God in all things."

## The Company of Jesus

After a pilgrimage to the Holy Land, Ignatius studied — a grown man in the classrooms of Barcelona, Alcalá, Salamanca, and finally Paris, where he gathered six companions, among them Francis Xavier and Peter Faber. In 1534, at Montmartre, they vowed poverty, chastity, and obedience, and offered themselves to the pope for mission. In 1540 Pope Paul III approved the new Society of Jesus. As its first superior general, Ignatius governed from Rome — writing, as one historian put it, "as much by letter as by speech," sending Jesuits to the schools, the courts, and the farthest missions of the world.

## A Life Offered

Ignatius lived by the prayer he gave his followers: "Take, Lord, and receive all my liberty, my memory, my understanding, and my entire will." He died in Rome on 31 July 1556. With Francis Xavier he was canonized in 1622. His legacy — the Jesuits, the *Exercises*, the schools and universities, the missionary dynamism of the Counter-Reformation — shaped the Church for centuries, and his way of discernment remains a gift to every Christian seeking God's will in the ordinary choices of life.
