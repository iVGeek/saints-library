Of all the apostles, John is the one who seems to have understood best what he had seen. In his Gospel, written last, at the end of a long life, he does not begin with a stable or a genealogy but with a threshold in eternity: "In the beginning was the Word." The fisherman of Bethsaida, son of Zebedee and brother of James, had been called from his boat by the same voice that called Peter; but in the memory of the Church it was John who leaned on the breast of Jesus at the Last Supper, who alone of the Twelve stood beneath the cross, and to whom Jesus, in His last care for His mother, gave Mary as mother and received her as his own.

## The Disciple Whom Jesus Loved

He is never named in his own Gospel, only described: "the disciple whom Jesus loved." It is the loveliest title ever claimed by a writer, and it fits the book he left us — the Gospel in which love is the whole argument, the light, the bread, the vine, the shepherd, and the very name of God. He writes of signs no other evangelist records: the water made wine, the man born blind, the raising of Lazarus, the washing of feet. And he writes of the Word made flesh, "full of grace and truth," a phrase that could only have been written by a man who had spent his life remembering the sound of that voice.

## The Eagle and the Isle

The tradition of the East gives John a long life at Ephesus, where he cared for the Mother of God and wrote his Gospel, his letters, and — on the island of Patmos, where he was exiled — the Book of Revelation, the apocalypse of the eagle's eye. The artists gave him the eagle, the creature that flies highest and sees farthest, for the height of his Gospel's vision; the legend gives him a chalice of poison that could not harm him, for the strength of a love that overcame death. He died in peace at a great age — the only apostle who did not die by the sword — and the Church keeps his feast on December 27, in the octave of Christmas, because the man who wrote "the Word became flesh" is the one who best explains what was born in the stable.
