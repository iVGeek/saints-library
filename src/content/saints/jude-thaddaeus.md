Jude is the saint of last resorts — the apostle the whole world invokes when all other doors have closed. In the Gospels he appears once, at the Last Supper, in one of the most tender exchanges of the New Testament: "Lord, how is it that You will manifest Yourself to us, and not to the world?" And Jesus answers him: "If anyone loves Me, he will keep My word, and My Father will love him, and We will come to him and make our home with him." The apostle who asked that question, the question of intimacy with God, spent the rest of his life announcing the answer.

## The Apostle of the East

The New Testament calls him "Jude of James" — and also "Judas, not the Iscariot," to distinguish him from the betrayer; his Letter begins by calling himself "a servant of Jesus Christ and brother of James." Tradition makes him, with his brother James the Less and the Lord Himself, a kinsman of Jesus. After Pentecost he is said to have preached with the apostle Simon in Mesopotamia and Persia, through the lands of the East, founding churches that survive to this day. He left behind one brief letter in the canon — a handful of verses of warning and comfort against false teachers, written to keep the faith "once for all delivered to the saints."

## The Patron of Impossible Causes

He died a martyr, traditionally in Persia or Beirut, killed with the club or halberd that artists give him; the flame above his head is the flame that, in the old tradition, descended on the apostles at Pentecost and rested visibly on him. For centuries his cult was modest — and then the modern devotion to him exploded: the faithful, finding in the name of this forgotten apostle the perfect confidant of their own forgotten hopes, began to invoke him for "impossible cases." His shrines are now everywhere, his novena one of the most prayed in the world. The Catholic Bookshop's novena prayer calls him "patron of hopeless cases" and the whole troubled world continues to lay its desperate causes at his feet — because the apostle who once asked Jesus about intimacy has, for two millennia, been the saint who brings God's mercy to those who have nowhere else to turn.
