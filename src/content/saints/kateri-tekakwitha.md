Kateri Tekakwitha is the first Native American saint, and her life was a small, quiet epic of faith under pressure. She was born in 1656 at Ossernenon on the Mohawk River, the daughter of a Mohawk chief and of Kahenta, an Algonquin Christian mother. When she was four, smallpox swept the village; she survived, scarred and almost blind, but her father, her mother, and her infant brother died. The orphaned girl was raised by an uncle, the head of the Turtle Clan, and a Catholic aunt — and the half-blind child, who could not see the sun without pain, grew up watching the missionaries from a distance, hearing the Jesuit stories of the God her mother had loved.

## The Lily Among Thorns

At twenty she was baptized, taking the name Catherine — Kateri in Mohawk — and her conversion cost her. Her relatives shunned her; the young men of the village mocked her; her uncle, in fury at her refusal to work on Sunday, stripped her of her clothes. She endured it all, praying in the fields, making small crosses of twigs in the woods where she went to be alone with God. In 1676 she escaped by canoe to Kahnawake, the Christian Mohawk settlement across the St. Lawrence, where the Jesuits gave her shelter and the Christian women of the mission took her in. There, in 1677, she made her great choice: a vow of perpetual virginity, the first consecration of a Native woman of North America, keeping Christ as her only spouse.

## The Lily of the Mohawks

She lived at Kahnawake for three years, poor, austere, and radiant — a girl the missionaries could not keep from prayer, who wove her own clothes, made a small hut in the woods for her devotions, and taught the children. She died on 17 April 1680, at twenty-four, and the witnesses said that in her last hour the smallpox scars faded from her face, leaving it beautiful. "She was the marvel of the mission," wrote her Jesuit biographer; within weeks her tomb was a shrine. Three centuries later, in 2012, Benedict XVI raised her to the altars in Rome — the first Native American saint, the Lily of the Mohawks, whose whole life had been the answer to the question of what a young woman can do with love in a hostile world: she can give it to God, and let Him keep it safe.
