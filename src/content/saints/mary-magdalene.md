Mary Magdalene — "Magdalene" meaning "from Magdala," the fishing town on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee — is the most prominent woman in the gospels after the Blessed Virgin, and the figure through whom the Church first learned of the Resurrection. In 2016 Pope Francis raised her memorial to the rank of a feast, in honor of her title "Apostle to the Apostles."

## The Woman Whom Jesus Called

The gospels tell of a woman from whom Jesus cast out seven demons, who followed him and ministered to him with her means. In the Western tradition, Luke's account of the penitent woman who washed Jesus' feet with her tears and anointed them with ointment has long been read together with Mary Magdalene — a conflation the Eastern churches did not make, and which modern scholarship treats with care. What is certain is the depth of her devotion: she appears among the faithful women at the crucifixion, while the disciples had fled, and she was first at the tomb.

## First Witness of the Resurrection

On the morning of Easter, it is Mary Magdalene who comes to the tomb, finds it empty, and runs to tell Peter and John. And it is to her that the Risen Christ first appears. He calls her by name — "Mary!" — and she answers "Rabbouni!" Then he sends her to the apostles with the message of his resurrection. On this account she is called in the tradition *Apostolorum Apostola*, the "Apostle to the Apostles": the one sent by Christ himself to announce the central mystery of the faith.

## The Woman of the Empty Tomb

In the art and prayer of the Church she is portrayed with the alabaster jar of ointment, with long flowing hair, and at the empty tomb greeting the Risen Lord. Legend carried her to Provence, where the shrine of Saint-Maximin-la-Sainte-Baume venerates her relics. In the East she is honored as an equal-to-the-apostles. Her love, her fidelity at the cross, and her courage at the dawn of Easter have made her for two thousand years the patroness of all who repent, all who love deeply, and all who are sent to speak of the One who calls them by name.
