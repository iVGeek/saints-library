Mary of Nazareth is the woman of whom the whole Christian story begins. She is the Virgin of the Annunciation, the one who said yes: "I am the handmaid of the Lord; let it be done to me according to your word." The angel Gabriel came to her in Nazareth, a village of no importance in Galilee, and told her that she would bear the Son of the Most High; and she, a girl of perhaps fourteen, without understanding but without refusal, consented — and the Word became flesh.

## The Mother of God

The Church calls her Theotokos, the God-bearer, and in that title lies the whole mystery of the Incarnation: God took His flesh from her. She carried Him in her womb, nursed Him at her breast, and raised Him in the carpenter's house at Nazareth. At the wedding at Cana she told the servants, "Do whatever He tells you" — the last recorded words of Mary in the Gospels, and perhaps the best summary of her life. She stood at the foot of the cross, where Jesus gave her to John and John to her, making her the mother of the beloved disciple and of every Christian who stands beneath the cross. And she was there at Pentecost, in the upper room, with the apostles, when the Holy Spirit descended.

## The Assumption

The end of Mary's earthly life is told in the tradition of the Dormition — the "falling asleep" — and the Assumption: she died and was taken up body and soul into heaven, where she sits with her Son, crowned with twelve stars. The feast of her birth is kept on September 8; the feast of her Assumption on August 15; and every Christmas, every Easter, every time the name of Jesus is spoken, the Church remembers the woman who made it possible — who said yes, and whose yes gave the world its Savior.
