Michael — the name means "Who is like God?" — is the only archangel named in the New Testament and the most frequently invoked guardian in Catholic tradition. The Scriptures portray him as the leader of the heavenly host, the great prince who guards the people of God and defeats the dragon. In the Book of Daniel he stands beside the prophet's people; in the Letter of Jude he disputes with the devil, yet in reverence refuses to rebuke him on his own authority; in the Book of Revelation he leads the angels of heaven in the war against Satan.

## Guardian of the Church

From the earliest centuries Christians have turned to Michael as protector of the Church, of the faithful, and of the dying. He is honored as the patron of soldiers and police, of sailors and the sick, and — from a story in which he appeared as a wandering merchant — of grocers. His image, sword raised over a conquered dragon, is among the most common in Christian art, appearing over church doors, on baptismal fonts, and in icons of the last judgment, where he is shown weighing souls in his scales.

## The Prayer of Leo XIII

In 1886 Pope Leo XIII, who had reportedly received a vision of the evil to befall the Church in the coming century, composed the Prayer to Saint Michael and ordered it recited after Low Mass — a custom observed until the liturgical reforms of the Second Vatican Council. Modern popes have renewed the devotion: Saint John Paul II urged the faithful to pray it, and Pope Francis has recommended it at times of crisis. The feast of the archangels Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael is kept together on September 29, and the Church prays that Michael, who cast down the ancient serpent, may ever defend the altar of God.
