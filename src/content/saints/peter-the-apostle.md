Simon, a fisherman from Bethsaida on the Sea of Galilee, was the first of the apostles to be called by Jesus and the one on whom Christ chose to build his Church. His story is one of the most human in the gospels: impulsive, faithful, brave, and broken — called a rock and yet capable of the deepest failure, restored by mercy and made the foundation of the new people of God.

## The Call and the Confession

Jesus found Simon by the shore of the lake and told him to put out into deep water. After the miraculous catch, Simon fell at his knees: "Depart from me, Lord, for I am a sinful man." Instead, Jesus made him his disciple and gave him a new name: Peter, the Rock. When Jesus asked the twelve whom they believed him to be, Peter answered for them all — "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God" — and Jesus replied: "You are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church... I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven." This confession and this promise are the foundation of the Church's teaching on the papacy.

## The Denial and the Restoration

Peter's courage carried him onto the water toward Christ — and into the darkness of denial. Warned by Jesus that he would deny him three times before the cock crowed, Peter protested his loyalty, then at the trial of Jesus stood by the fire in the high priest's courtyard and swore three times that he did not know the man. The gospels record that when the cock crowed, Peter went out and wept bitterly. His restoration came at dawn on the shore of Tiberias, where the Risen Christ asked him three times, "Simon, son of John, do you love me?" — three times undoing the threefold denial — and three times commanded him, "Feed my sheep."

## The Rock in Rome

After Pentecost, Peter became the leading preacher of the young Church, opening it to the Gentiles at the baptism of Cornelius. Tradition holds that he went to Rome, the capital of the empire, and there, during the persecution of Nero, was crucified head downward at his own request, declaring himself unworthy to die as his Master had. He was buried on the Vatican Hill, where the great basilica of Saint Peter now stands over his tomb. The Church commemorates him with Saint Paul on June 29, and his keys remain the emblem of the papacy — a sign, as the tradition has always taught, that the Church stands not on human strength but on the confession and mercy of the Lord.
