Padre Pio was the most extraordinary priest of the twentieth century — and the most ordinary. For fifty years he got up before dawn, said Mass at five, and sat in the confessional for twelve, fourteen hours a day, hearing the sins and sorrows of pilgrims who had traveled from every continent to kneel at his grille. He was a Capuchin friar of San Giovanni Rotondo, in the dry south of Italy, born Francesco Forgione at Pietrelcina in 1887; and from the day in 1918 when he received the wounds of Christ on his hands and feet and side, he was a living riddle — a man of miracles whom nobody could entirely explain, and a man of prayer whom everybody could understand.

## The Stigmata and the Sacrament

The wounds never closed. For fifty years he bled from his hands, and for fifty years the Church tested and doubted and finally accepted him. Doctors examined him; skeptics accused him of fraud; the Vatican at one point restricted his ministry — and through all of it he prayed, suffered, and forgave. His biographers record his constant companion: a battle of the spirit, the "assaults of the devil" he said God permitted, so that he might be purified. Yet the crowds who came to him came for mercy, and he gave it without measure — his confessionals were open in every language, and his famous advice to the anxious is still on the lips of millions: "Pray, hope, and don't worry."

## The House for the Relief of Suffering

His love took brick and mortar in 1956, when he opened the Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza — the House for the Relief of Suffering — a modern hospital built on the hill at San Giovanni Rotondo, where the sick are treated as Christ would treat them. He died in 1968, and the wounds that had bled for fifty years were gone from his hands. Millions had come to him in life; today San Giovanni Rotondo is one of the greatest shrines of the Church. John Paul II canonized him in 2002 before a quarter-million people — a sign, the pope said, that the cross and the mercy of Christ are not things of the past, but are given again to every age in the lives of His saints.
