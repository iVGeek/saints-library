Married, a mother, and living in Kemet, Upper Egypt, Cleopatra witnessed the martyrdom of Saint Varus of Kemet, and developed a great devotion to him. When her husband died, Cleopatra took both his body and the relics of Saint Varus to Daraa, Syria for burial in the family tomb. When her son John, died in 319, Cleopatra prayed for the intercession of Varus to bring John back to her; instead, she received a vision of Varus and John together in heaven, and was comforted to know that her son was with God. She then built a chapel to enshrine the relics of Varus, and buried her son next to him. The relics became locally famous for healing miracles, and the chapel became a pilgrimage site. Cleopatra gave away her wealth, and spent her days praying, fasting and ministering to pilgrims.
