Born to a wealthy and well-connected family, Eptadius was a well-educated young man known for his serious nature, maturity and learning. Just before his wedding at age 20, he was struck by a severe fever; during the illness, the three pious women who cared for him brought him back to health, and to an active faith. Bishop Flavian of Autun, France encouraged Eptadius to study for the priesthood, but the young man did not consider himself worthy, and became a hermit in the forested area known as Cervon (deer mountain). His reputation for wisdom and piety attracted so many would-be spiritual students that he founded a Benedictine monastery for them; it later became known as the Saint-Eptade de Cervon abbey, Eptadius served as its first abbot, and was ordained a priest. King Clovis tried to appoint Father Eptadius as bishop of Auxerre, France, but he refused, preferring to serve in the abbey. Beyond the wisdom and piety, Eptadius was known for his charity and work to ransom Christians from prison or slavery.
