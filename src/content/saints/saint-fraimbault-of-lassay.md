Born to the Frankish nobility, the son of a count. At age 15, against his own objections, he followed his father's orders and became a servant in the court of King Childebert. He found that he despised life in court, referring to it as a “barbaric environment”, and left to become a cave hermit in Ivry-sur-Seine, devoted to meditation and penance; legend says that a fountain of water sprang up at the entrance to keep visitors away. He entered the abbey of Micy c.518, where he became a spiritual student of Saint Maximinus of Micy and served as porter. He studied, was ordained a priest, and trained in preaching and evangelism.
In 532, at the invitation of Saint Innocent of Le Mans, and several brothers, including Saint Constantine of Javron, he established a group of hermits, then an oratory, and then a monastery in the area of Maine that became the village of Saint-Fraimbault-de-Prières, France; another area of his work led to the village of Lassay-les-Chateaux, France. He used these as bases to evangelize the region, and became known as a miracle worker. The village of Villers-Saint-Frambourg, France is named for him.
