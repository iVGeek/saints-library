Hermit in the forests in the region of Berrien and Huelgoat in Brittany about whom little history but many legends have survived.
&bull; Herbot's reputation for holiness and his skill as a preacher attracted many would-be spiritual students, including the men of nearby villages. He converted many, and all the local men became so interested in the faith that they neglected their homes and fields and work. This cause the local women to remonstrate against him, demand that he stop, demand that he leave, and even to throw stones at him. They never hurt him, but the region is littered to this day with the granite stones they threw, and the farmers never seem to clear the fields of all of them.
&bull; The lord of Rusquec gave Herbot two oxen to help build a hermitage. The saint became good friends with the animals, could speak their language, conversed with them, and preached to all the local livestock.
&bull; If a farmer had a sick cow, prayed over it, asking for Saint Herbot's intercession, and the animal recovered, tradition said that the farmer had to cut off the end of the animal's tail and present it a the chapel in thanks. If a sick horse recovered due to Herbot's intercession, bundles of its tail hair would be presented. The hair was sold for making brushes, or used by sailors as caulking in ships in hope that it would preserve them from cannon ball damage.
&bull; Because of his connection to cattle and livestock in general, folk traditions led to invoking his intercession to make cows give milk, even if they had gone dry, and for butter to set when it just wouldn't break.
