Bishop of Sabiona, Italy. Possible bishop of Brixen, Italy.
During a famine in 424, Bishop Lucanus permitted the consumption of dairy products in his diocese during Lent, a violation of fasting rules. Enemies denounced him to the Vatican, and Pope Celestine I ordered him to Rome to explain himself.
&bull; On the road, Lucanus and his servant were set upon by a bear, which killed and ate the horse Lucanus had been riding. Lucanus, being an old man on a trip to the Vatican, ordered the bear, in God's name, to take the horse's place; the bear then allowed itself to be saddled, and the bishop rode it the rest of the way to Rome.
&bull; The little party stopped overnight at an inn in Spoleto, Italy where they found the landlord's wife bedridden with dropsy; the bishop cured her by praying over her.
&bull; Back on the road, Lucanus ordered a flock of partridges to stay together and fly ahead of him to Rome. He then gave them to the Pope as a gift.
&bull; Just before he arrived at the papal palace, the bishop got caught in a rain shower. He did not want to wear the soaked outer cloak to his audience with the pope, but could find no place to hang it up. So, he draped it over a ray of sunshine that was streaming through a window, and it hung there to dry while he met the pope.
The Pope immediately saw that Lucanus was a holy man, obviously in God's graces, and understood that the disregard for fasting rules had been done to save the bishop's people. He accepted Lucanus's apology, blessed him, and sent him on his way.
At home his enemies refused to give up, and simply resorted to using force to drive Lucanus into exile. He took refuge in the Agordo valley area in Belluno, Italy and lived the rest of his life in a cave in an area later called Valle di San Lugano in his honor. He was befriended by Saint Avatia in Listolade, his reputation for holiness and miracle work spread, he evangelized and healed throughout the area.
