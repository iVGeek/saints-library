Appointed imperial Roman prefect of Gaul by Emperor Diocletian in the late 3rd century, Rictiovarus was known for his hatred and persecution of Christians, including ordering the deaths of

Alexander of Trier
Boniface of Trier
Crispin
Crispinian
Fuscian of Amiens
Gentian of Amiens
Justus of Beauvais
Macra of Rheims
Palmatius of Trier and companions
Quintinus of Augusta Veromanduorum
Rufinus of Soissons
Thyrsus of Trier
Valerius of Soissons
Victoricus of Amiens
the 23 Martyrs of Campania

Legend says that during the execution of Saint Crispin and Saint Crispinian, he was moved by the miracles involving them, converted to Christianity, and was martyred himself. There are also stories that his failure to kill the martyrs led him kill himself, a standard practice for imperial officials who failed, and he either jumped into the fire or drowned himself. There are also legends in the German Saarland that Rictiovarus refuses to stay in his tomb, haunts the Varuswald forest, and/or leads a band of vengeful, flying, marauding ghosts.
Much of this sounds like folk tales piled on top of folk tales. Modern scholarship indicates that he may not even have existed, there are no contemporary stories about his conversion, and his story seems an instance of fiction confused as history.
