A missionary priest from east of the region, Theodulus became the first known bishop of Sion (Sitten), Switzerland, consecrated in the 340's, and serving over 40 years. He attended the Council of Aquileia in 381, and was signatory of the documents of the Synod of Milan in 390 that condemned the heresy of the monk Jovinian. He opposed the spread of the Arian heresy.
Bishop Theodulus located the tomb of Saint Maurice. He founded the Abbey of Saint–Maurice in Agaunum, Switzerland, the first abbey north of the Alps, where he brought together the local pious hermits to live in communal life, and enshrined all the relics of the Theban Legion that he could locate.
Legend says that the pope gave Theodulus a large church bell as a gift, and the saint forced the devil to carry it home for him, particularly through a section of the Alps that became known as the Theodul Pass in the bishop's honour. The incident led to his patronage of bells, bell makers, bell ringers, etc.
Late in Theodulus's life, there was a frost that destroyed the wine harvest in the Valais region. The bishop set up a cask in the wine cellar of Saint Peter's church in Sion, Switzerland, and told his parishioners that any could come and get from it all they needed to drink. He ordered them to never open the cask, and frowned on any of the wine being sold. It lasted for years, and became a staple for the area poor. After his death, some of the locals broke open the cask to see how it had provided so much wine. They found the barrel dry, with a single bunch of grapes hanging inside it. As they watched, the grapes turned to dust, the cask fell apart, and that was the end of the miracle.
