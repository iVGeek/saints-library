Born to the Cornish nobility, the son of Lord Arastagn. Rector of Brasparts in Brittany (in modern France) where he worked with Saint Joavan of Brittany. Hermit and monk, and often depicted as an abbot.
A chapel was later built on the site of his hermitage; the chapel a place of treatment (or imprisonment) of people who had been bitten by dogs or wolves, and were feared to have contracted rabies. Keys were later given to pilgrims as a rememberance of the pilgrimage, and to protect against rabies.
