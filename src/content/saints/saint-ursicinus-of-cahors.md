Monk. Abbot. Chancellor of Queen Ultragotha. Ninth bishop of Cahors, France in the early 580's. Saint Gregory of Tours mentions him in several writings. At one point he came into conflict with a fellow bishop who accused Ursicinus of taking charge of several parishes outside of the diocese of Cahors – and the tithes that came with them; he was cleared of the charges by a council of bishops. A year later, Ursicinus was excommunicated and sentenced to a three-year penance by another council for the “crime” of welcoming a man who was rebelling against the ruling Merovingian dynasty. We lose track of Ursicinus after this, but surviving sacramentaries and psalters in later centuries show there was devotion to him, feast celebrated on 13 December, villages named for him, and churches in the Cahors diocese dedicated to him.
