Born a prince, the son of Hamazasp and Dustr; his maternal grandfather was Isaac, the Armenian katholikos or leader of the regional Church, who saw to the religious training of young Vardan. A career soldier, Vardan became supreme commander of the armies of Armenia in 432. Married and the father of one, Susanna, who grew up to be a martyr in her turn.
On Holy Saturday, 12 April 450, Vardan denounced the Zoroastrian religion of the Persians, who were insisting on decrees of faith and loyalty from all regional leaders. He led the Armenian army against the Persians at the Battle of Avarayr on 26 May 451. Though he was unable to win victory on the battlefield, the resulting negotiations secured the Armenians' right to practice Christianity. Dying in the field, fighting for the faith, Vardan and his fellow soldiers are considered martyrs, and Vardan a national hero.
