Almost everything we know of Saint Scholastica comes from one page of Saint Gregory the Great's *Dialogues* — and that page is among the most beloved in all of Christian literature. She was the twin sister of Saint Benedict, born at Nursia around the year 480, consecrated to God from her youth, and the foundress of the first women's monastery known to the Benedictine family, at Plombariola near Monte Cassino.

## The Storm of Love

Once a year, Gregory tells us, the brother and sister met at a house between their two monasteries to speak together of the things of God. On her last visit, as the evening wore on, Scholastica sensed that the conversation was her farewell, and she begged Benedict to remain through the night. He refused — his rule, he said, would not permit him to stay outside his monastery. She bowed her head in prayer, and a storm of rain and thunder such as the region had never known broke upon them, so that Benedict could not go out. When he reproached her, she answered: "I asked you a favor and you would not listen to me; so I asked my Lord and he has heard me."

Three days later, in his monastery, Benedict saw his sister's soul rise to heaven in the form of a dove. He had her body laid in the tomb he had prepared for himself, and the twins rest together to this day. The point of the story is not the weather but the love: a woman who loved her brother, and her God, more than she loved any rule — even a rule she herself honored perfectly.

## The First Monastic for Women

Before Scholastica, the Rule of her brother had been written for men. Her community adapted the Benedictine observance to the life of women, and through her the monastery became the school of sanctity for generations of nuns in the West. Her patronage — of nuns, of monastic life, and of prayer that will not take "no" for an answer — flows from that single, luminous scene: the sister who, by love, held the whole law of obedience in her hands and still bowed to the greater law of charity.
