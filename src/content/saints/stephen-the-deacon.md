The Church's first martyr is the subject of its first martyrdom story, and the account in the Acts of the Apostles reads like a trial in heaven. Stephen was a Hellenist Jew of the earliest Jerusalem church, "full of faith and of the Holy Spirit," chosen by the apostles with six others to serve the daily distribution to the widows — the very origin of the diaconate. He was, the text says, "full of grace and power, doing great wonders and signs among the people," and his preaching drew the fierce hostility of the synagogue leaders, who could not answer his wisdom.

## The Speech Before the Sanhedrin

Arrested and brought before the council, Stephen delivered the longest speech in the Acts of the Apostles — a recital of Israel's whole history of God's faithfulness and human resistance, ending with the charge: "You stiff-necked people, you always resist the Holy Spirit." The council was cut to the heart and ground their teeth at him. But Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, looked up to heaven and saw the glory of God — and Jesus "standing at the right hand of God," the Son of Man risen to welcome His first witness. They dragged him out of the city and stoned him.

## The Prayer of the First Martyr

As the stones fell, Stephen prayed in the exact words of his Master on the cross: "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit," and then, on his knees, "Lord, do not hold this sin against them." The witnesses laid their cloaks at the feet of a young man named Saul — the same Saul who was on his way to become Paul, whose conversion the Church has always loved to see as the first fruit of Stephen's prayer. The day after Christmas, the Church sets his feast — the protomartyr follows the birth of the One for whom he died — and honors in him the truth that the first Christian blood shed in the west was shed praying for forgiveness, in a death that looked exactly like the death it imitated.
