Thomas Aquinas was born around 1225 at Roccasecca, the youngest son of a noble family of the Kingdom of Naples. His life looks, on the surface, like the biography of a genius who simply sat down and thought. It was in fact a war. His family, hoping for a brilliant ecclesiastical career for him, resisted his vocation to the new Order of Preachers so fiercely that his brothers kidnapped him and shut him in a castle for a year. They sent a woman to tempt him; he drove her out with a burning brand. When they finally released him, Thomas went quietly back to the Dominicans, and from that moment his life belongs to the order and to the Church.

## The Ox and the Eagle

In Paris and Cologne he studied under Albert the Great, who silenced the mockers who called the quiet giant the "dumb ox": "We call him the dumb ox," Albert said, "but he will low so loud his bellowing will be heard the whole world over." It was Thomas who did the unthinkable for the thirteenth century: he took the newly rediscovered philosophy of Aristotle — long suspected as the ally of heresy — and showed that it could be the servant of faith. Faith and reason, he taught, cannot truly conflict, because both come from the same God. In the *Summa Theologiae*, his masterwork of 1265 to 1273, he built a cathedral of thought in which every question a Christian could ask finds its ordered place, from the existence of God to the smallest act of virtue.

## The Silence at the End

The hagiography says that near the end of his life, at the altar, Thomas was granted such a vision of God that he laid down his pen and refused to write another word: "I can write no more," he told his friend. "All that I have written seems to me as straw beside what I have seen." He died at the abbey of Fossanova in 1274, on his way to the Council of Lyons, still in his forties. The Church has called him the Angelic Doctor, has made him the common doctor of her schools, and has set his feast on January 28 — but his truest memorial is every Catholic classroom, every inquiry that begins in faith and does not fear the truth.
