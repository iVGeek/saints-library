Ordained a priest of the archdiocese of Poznan, Poland on 10 June 1933. During World War II he was imprisoned in the former Bruczków monastery, then the Buchenwald and Dachau concentration camps; he survived over five years in the camps, being freed by American troops on 29 April 1945. He returned to parish ministry in Poland, but in 1950 was imprisoned for ten months by Communist authorities for refusing to cooperate with them against the Church. He was later appointed national director for pastoral care of women by the Polish Episcopal Conference. Appointed president of the Synodal Commission for the Apostolate of the Laity in 1966. During all his assignments, Father Aleksander found time to regularly preach retreats, and help found parish groups for the laity to support each other. He had a deep devotion to Saint Therese of Lisieux, and was known for his deep prayer life; he recited the Way of the Cross every day, was a sought after confessor and spiritual director, and served over 50 years in the priesthood.
