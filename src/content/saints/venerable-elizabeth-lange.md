To escape harsh racial discrimination, Elizabeth's family moved to South Carolina in the United States in 1813. Because of the racial laws there, Elisabeth moved on to Baltimore, Maryland. There she found that education of black children ranged from none to completely outlawed. With three friends, she started a free school, the Saint Frances Academy, based from her home. This group, with the spiritual direction of the Sulpician priest Father James Nicholas Joubert, became the core of a new religious order, the Institute of the Oblate Sisters of Providence in 1829; this was the first black Roman Catholic order in the United States. Elisabeth took the name Sister Mary, served as the Superior of the Sisters, as novice mistress, and is considered their founder.
