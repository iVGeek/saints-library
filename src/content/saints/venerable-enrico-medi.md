The son of Arturo Medi, a physician, and Maria Luisa Mei. When Enrico was about 3 years old, his family moved to Belvedere Ostrense, Italy, and then to Rome, Italy when he was about nine. While in high school and college, he co-founded the Student Missionary League, and served as its first president. In 1932, at age 21, he received a degree in physics, having written a thesis on the nature of the neutron. He earned a teaching qualification in 1937, and was chosen chair of experimental physics at the University of Palermo in 1942. He did early work on radar and the study of what became known as the Van Allen belts that protect the earth, but the Fascists repressed his studies. He was married, and the father of six girls.
Enrico entered politics in the post-World War II era. He was elected to the Constituent Assembly in 1946 as a member of the Christian Democrat party, and then to the new parliment in 1948; he served until 1953, and was known for his support of Italian regional autonomy, and the peaceful use of atomic energy. Director of the National Institute of Geophysics in Rome in 1949, and then chair of geophysics there in 1955. Vice-president of the European Atomic Energy Community in 1958. In the 1950s he hosted one of the first popular science television programs, The Adventures of Science. Appointed to the Lay Council for the Vatican City State in 1966. On 20–21 July 1969 he was one of the commentators for the Italian broadcast of the moon landing. Worked to support a 1970 referendum to repeal liberal divorce laws in Italy. Elected to the city council of Rome in 1971. Elected to the Chamber of Deputies in 1972.
Along with all his secular, political and academic pursuits and success, Enrico worked to promote the faith, and to support institutions that brought the faith to people in concrete terms. He worked with the Jesuit-led Better World movement, with the House for the Relief of Suffering in Rome, spoke on ethics, and the connection between theology, the faith and the sciences. He attended Mass daily, prayed the Rosary, spoke of his faith, and never forgot that the success he had achieved needed to be used for the service of his fellow man.
