Lifelong lay woman of the archdiocese of Mallorca, Spain, she was born to a wealthy family who had immigrated to Spain from the south of France; Francisca was the only child in the family to survive to adulthood. She was educated at a school run by the Daughters of Maria Scolopie, and became a poet and newspaper writer. An officer in Catholic Action, she was noted for her dedication to the Eucharist, her Marian spirituality, and her work to support the spiritual lives of young people. She devoted several years in her 30's to caring for her parents as their health deteriorated and the family became increasingly poor. She was diagnosed with a brain tumour in 1951 and her own health deteriorated over her remaining three years as she increasingly lost her mobility and her sight, but never her patience, piety, or desire to help others.
