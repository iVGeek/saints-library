Son of Thomas Panicker and Annamma Panicker. Attending Madras Christian College as an ordained deacon in the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, he earned degrees in economics and Indian history. Ordained a priest on 15 August 1908. Founded the Order of the Imitation of Christ (also known as the Bethany Ashram) in 1919. Consecrated as a bishop on 1 May 1925. On 20 September 1930, he and several others converted from the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church to union with Rome, which led to the formation of the Syro-Malankara Catholic Church. Titular archbishop of Phasis on 13 February 1932. Appointed Syro-Malankarese archbishop of Trivandrum, India on 11 June 1932. Founded the Sisters of the Imitation of Christ (known as the Bethany Madhom). Founded Mar Ivanios College, approximately 50 other schools, and established about 150 parishes and missions in the region.
