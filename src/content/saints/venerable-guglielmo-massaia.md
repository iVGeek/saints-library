Member of the Order of Friars Minor Capuchin, taking the name Guglielmo in honour of his deceased brother who had been a canon of the cathedral in Asti, Italy, and had served as Lorenzo's spiritual teacher; he made his solemn profession on 8 September 1825. Ordained a priest in the diocese of Vercelli, Italy on 16 June 1832. Spiritual director of a hospital in Turin, Italy from 1834 to 1836. Confessor and spiritual advisor to Saint Joseph Benedict Cottolengo. Preacher, lecturer in theology, and confessor to Prince Victor Emmanuel, the future king of Italy.
Members of the royal family thought highly of Father Guglielmo, and were willing to use their influence to have him chosen a bishop, but he was more interested in missionary work. Chosen Vicar Apostolic of Galla, Ethiopia and titular bishop of Casius on 12 May 1846, he served in Abyssinia for 34 years. In 1880, his health failing, Guglielmo was forced to return to Italy where Pope Leo XIII made him the titular archbishop of Stauropolis on 2 August 1881. Elevated to Cardinal-Priest of Santi Vitale, Valeria, Gervasio e Protasio on 10 November 1884 by Pope Leo XIII.
