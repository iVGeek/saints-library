The fifth of eight children of Vartan Zohrabian, an Armenian baker, and his wife Sara, née Hovhannessian. Cirillo joined the Franciscan Capuchins in Constantinople in 1898; he took the name Guregh (Cyril) at his profession. Ordained a priest on 12 May 1904. He founded a church, a school, and an orphanage in Erzurum. He was assigned to Constantinople during World War I, and so survived the Armenian genocide. He taught at Saint Joseph's College, run by the Brothers of the Christian Schools, in Kadikòy, and served as rector of the College of San Luigi dei Francesi of Constantinople, but was removed from those positions by the Turks, and sent to an internment camp in 1916 where he ministered to Italian and Greek prisoners. He was released in 1920, but imprisoned again on 7 March 1923 by the Turks and repeatedly tortured for celebrating Mass, which they had outlawed. He was sentenced to death for his faith, but the sentence was commuted to exile. He ministered to Armenian refugees on Corfù, and was chosen Armenian Ordinary in Greece in 1925, but he declined, fearing it would cause conflict with the local Orthodox community. Patriarchal Vicar of Jazeera, Syria, and an active missionary from 1938 to 1953. Auxiliary bishop of Cilicia, Lebanon, and titular bishop of Acilisene on 29 August 1940. He founded more churches and schools, set up charities and youth ministry, and spent his life caring for his people. Participated in four sessions of the Second Vatican Council.
