The son of Francisco Antonio Lázaro Molinero Román and Ángela María Francisca Novillo y López, Ismael was baptized at the age of five days, made his first Communion on 11 June 1925 at age 8, and was confirmed by Blessed Narciso de Esténaga y Echevarría. In 1931, at age 14, he dropped out of school and went to work as a store clerk to help his family. He joined Catholic Action in Tomelloso, Spain in 1933, and over the next year or so became active in their work, in his parish, in visiting the local hospital, and in his faith in general; he became the treasurer of his Catholic Action chapter. He made a spiritual retreat in the Ciudad Real seminary in April 1935 that was led by Father José Sánchez Oliva.
On 26 July 1936, at the start of the Spanish Civil War, Communist anti–Catholic militiamen attacked the church in Tomelloso, burning all images; Ismael managed to save some, which led to threats from the militia, which led to him hiding out in Ruidera with his brother Luis. The militia later murdered Ismael's spiritual director and burned the area churches. In 1937 he was press-ganged into joining Republican Army and sent into combat in Teruel, Spain. During fighting in Alfambra, Spain, Ismael laid down his weapon, held up his miraculous medal, and waited to die, preferring that to killing. Instead he was imprisoned and sent to a concentration camp. There he developed a friendship with the camp chaplain, did what he could to encourage the faith and his fellow prisoners, and died of natural causes.
