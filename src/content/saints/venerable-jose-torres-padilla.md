The son of Francisco de Torres Bauta and María Padilla Cabeza, José was baptized at the age of six days in the parish of Our Lady of the Assumption. He was orphaned young, with both parents dying of natural causes on the same day; he and his three brothers were raised by relatives.
José had always felt a call to religious life, and never mentioned any vocation but the priesthood. At age 16 he moved to Tenerife, Spain to begin his studies at the University of San Fernando de la Laguna. He continued in Valencia, Spain in 1834, and then the seminary in Seville, Spain in 1835; ordained a priest in the archdiocese of Seville in 1836.
He was known for a devotion to the Eucharist, and was a member of the Royal Sevillian Congregation of Light and of the Candle for the Blessed Sacrament. Professor of theology at the Seville seminary beginning in 1847. Beginning around 1862, he became the confessor and spiritual director of Saint Angela of the Cross Guerrero, and helped her found the Sisters of the Company of the Cross. At the order of Pope Pius IX, he served as pontifical consultant and theologian to the First Vatican Council. Canon of the cathedral of Seville in 1872.
