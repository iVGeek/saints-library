Born to a Jewish family that had converted to Catholicism. Juan studied at the University of Alcalá de Henares from 1579 to 1582 when he became a Discalced Carmelite novice; he made his profession on 30 January 1583. He continued his studies in Alcalá, Spain, in Genoa, Italy, and was ordained a priest in 1589.
In 1593, Father Juan was part of the Carmelite Chapter in which the Discalced Carmelites separated from the Calced. He became novice master in Genoa for several years, and in 1597 was transferred to Rome, Italy to work on the committee writing the constitutions of the Discalced Carmelites. Vicar of the convent of Santa Maria della Scala in Rome from 1600 to 1611. Chosen General Definitor and Master of Novices for the Order in 1605. Chosen Definitor and Procurator General of the Order in 1608. Among his 27 books, he wrote a biography of Saint Teresa of Avila in 1609, and worked for her beatification. Chosen Superior General of the Discalced Carmelites in 1611, he promoted the start of missionary work by the Order. In his last years, he was too ill to travel and governed the Order by an endless series of correspondence.
