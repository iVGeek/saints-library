Ordained a priest on 20 December 1834, he continued his education and earned a degree in theology at the Royal University in Naples, Italy. Father Luigi served in chapels along the waterfront, caring for sailors and fishermen, and with a ministry to the sick and to prisoners. Rector of Saint Mary of Egypt church in the Pizzofalcone area of Naples. Curate treasurer of Our Lady of the Sea church. He preached missions throughout the Campania region for several years. Chosen bishop of Crotone, Italy on 18 March 1852, but had to withdraw from service after several months due to ill health. Recovered, he was chosen bishop of Telese o Cerreto Sannita, Italy on 6 April 1853. In 1860, during the turmoil of Italian unification, Bishop Luigi was forced into exile from his diocese; at one point he was imprisoned for six weeks, falsely accused of plotting with royalists against the government. He was finally able to return to Cerreto Sannita on 15 September 1865. To help with rebuilding the Church after the political disruptions, he dedicated himself to serving the poor, supporting his priests, and working for more vocations to the seminary. Served as a Council Father at the First Vatican Council in 1869 and 1870.
