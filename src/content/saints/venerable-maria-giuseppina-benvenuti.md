Kidnapped as a child and sold by Arab slavers, Zeinab was ransomed out of slavery by Father Niccolò Olivieri, and taken to Italy. Baptized Catholic on 24 September 1856, she took the name Maria Giuseppina Benvenuti; the 'Benvenuti' part was her godmother's last name. Educated by the Franciscans, she was a skilled musician and noted organist. She joined the Poor Clare nuns in 1874, making her profession in 1876. Her house, the Belvedere monastery, was suppressed in 1894, and she moved to the Serra de' Conti monastery where she served as vicar, then novice mistress, and finally abbess. She was known as intelligent, loving and wise, spent much time in contemplation of the mysteries of the faith, and had devotions to Saint Joseph (she sought to emulate his complete availability to God's instruction), Saint Francis of Assisi (followed his love of the crucifix), Saint Clare of Assisi (the ardor of her prayer life) and Saint Cecilia (for her connection between music and God). She became blind in her last years, which seemed to increase her mystical experience of Communion.
