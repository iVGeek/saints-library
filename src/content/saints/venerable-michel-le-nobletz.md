One of eleven children born to a family in the minor French nobility, the son of Hervé Le Nobletz and Françoise de Lesvern; his father was a royal notary. Michel studied at small local schools, then at the Jesuit college in Agen, France, and at the Sorbonne, then spent a year living as a hermit in a cell on the beach in Plouguerneau, France, discerning his vocation.
Priest of the diocese of Quimper-Léon, France. He was very active in the Counter-Reformation, preaching against the vices of the rich and ruling classes, working to bring the Church back as an element of culture and every-day life in Brittany. He developed the use of a graphic form called the taolennù or mission tables to represent and show symbols to support the points of his sermons. Father Michel re-wrote the lyrics of popular, sometimes bawdy, songs to make them more edifying, which led to some opposition from authorities who did not understand what he was doing. He enlisted the help of pious local women, including his sisters Anne and Marguerite, which caused some opposition from people who did not believe that such un-worldly women should be in the rough, working-class areas of Brittany. His preaching led to him being exiled from Douamenez, but he continued his work from Conquet, limited, and eventually paralyzed, by what appears to have been Parkinson's disease.
He miraculously received knowledge of the birth of Blessed Julian Maunoir and knew that Julian would some day come to his aid; when Julian was ordained, he was assigned to Father Michel, and learned his ways of ministry and evangelization.
