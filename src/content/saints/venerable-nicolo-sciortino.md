The fifth of six children born to Domenico Sciortino and Ninfa Colletti, he was baptised on the day he was born and raised in a pious farm family; his father had taken minor orders. Nicolò grew up helping with the family flocks, and, feeling an early call to religion, spent his time in the fields in prayer. He joined the Capuchins at the convent of Monte San Giuliano on 24 April 1735 at age 30, and even as a novice was recognized as a model friar. Nicolò became a lay brother in the Order, taking the name Andrea. He served as a missionary to the Congo region from 1745 to 1762. When he returned to Europe, he met King Joseph I of Portugal who was so impressed with Andrea's piety that he tried to get him to stay in Lisbon. Brother Andrea was drawn back to life in the convent, and returned to Palermo where he lived in, and died in, the same cell in which Saint Bernardo da Corleone had died 105 years earlier.
