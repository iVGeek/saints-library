Born to the Portuguese nobility, the daughter of João de Saldanha Oliveira Juzarte Figueira e Sousa, 3rd Count of Rio Maior, and Isabel Maria of Prazeres de Sousa Botelho Mourão y Vasconcelos. Feeling a call to religious life and good works, in 1862 Teresa became the director of Santa Marta College for Poor Girls, working with the Daughters of Charity of São Vicente de Paulo. She founded the Poor Girls Protective Association to subsidize schools for women. In England, she joined the Dominicans, and then returned to Portugal. In 1868 she founded the Dominican Sisters of the Portuguese Congregation of Saint Catherine of Siena.
