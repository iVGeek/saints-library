The middle of three children born to a wealthy family; her father, Giuseppi, owned a chain of hotels in Egypt, and her mother, Giuseppina Viglini, was from an Italian upper class family. Teresa's mother insured the girl received an excellent education, spent time in prayer, and understood her responsibility to help the poor. Member of the Daughters of Mary. At her First Communion, age 12, Teresa felt a call to religious life, and she began to live a simpler life, even in boarding school. Her father died soon after, and the family moved to Rome, Italy; Teresa would sneak out of the house each morning to go to Mass, returning before the rest of the family were up. When she was old enough, she joined the Daughters of Mary, Help of Christian in Rome on 2 February 1901; she made her solemn profession in 1903. She served as a teacher, concentrating on literature, arts and music with girls that were deprived of such things in their lives, and begged alms to help support her house and the poor; she did well since she often begged at the homes of the wealthy people she knew from growing up in those circles. In 1905 she contracted tuberculosis, causing her to withdraw from a life in public, and to work in the laundry of her house until her health failed.
